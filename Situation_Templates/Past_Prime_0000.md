# Media Ecology, Joyce, and the Silhouette Void: 1922–2026 Field Notes

> *"One thing about which fish know exactly nothing is water, since they have no anti-environment which would enable them to perceive the element they live in."*  
> — Marshall McLuhan & Quentin Fiore, [*War and Peace in the Global Village*](https://wikipedia.org) (1968)

## Overview

This field notes document synthesizes a cross-disciplinary media-ecology map spanning early 20th-century literature (**1922–1939**), mid-century communication theory (**1968–1988**), tactile counter-environments (**1973–present**), and modern digital platform audits (**2017–2026**). It highlights how fast-moving algorithmic feeds reward reactivity while leaving foundational nonfiction and print resources unexamined—a blind spot designated as the **[Silhouette Void](https://criticalmediatheory.com)**.

---

## 1. The 1922–1939 Literary Foundation

Warnings regarding technological mediatization were articulated decades before the internet during the expansion of mass print, radio, and advertising:

* **James Joyce’s [*Ulysses*](https://wikipedia.org) (Feb 2, 1922):** Tracks Leopold Bloom through Dublin, showing how print headlines and ads influence public consciousness.
* **Sinclair Lewis’s [*Babbitt*](https://wikipedia.org) (Sept 14, 1922):** Satirizes standardized consumer culture where commercial goods and political routines replace genuine human experience.
* **James Joyce’s [*Finnegans Wake*](https://wikipedia.org) (May 4, 1939):** Uses cyclical history and recurring shockwaves (Thunderwords) to signal how technological shifts disrupt human language and identity. 
  * *Note on Pop Culture Overrides:* While **May 4** marks the publication of Joyce's text—which was famously analyzed by **Joseph Campbell** in *A Skeleton Key to Finnegans Wake* (1944)—it has been largely overshadowed in modern media by [*Star Wars* Day](https://starwars.com).

## 2. The Joseph Campbell & Jean Erdman Lineage

* **Partnership & Adaptation:** Dancer **Jean Erdman** and mythologist **Joseph Campbell** collaborated extensively, with Erdman adapting *Finnegans Wake* into the Obie-winning dance-drama ***The Coach with the Six Insides* (1962)**.
* **Mass Thinking vs. Independent Inquiry:** In *The Power of Myth* (1988), Campbell referenced Sinclair Lewis’s *Babbitt* to caution against herd consensus.
* **Hijacked Archetypes:** Commentators like executive producer **Joan Konner** have analyzed how political spectacle flips Campbell's Hero’s Journey, casting modern media figures into mythic roles while deploying automated systems (*[What Would Joseph Campbell Say About Donald Trump?](https://billmoyers.com)*, August 19, 2016).

## 3. Marshall McLuhan & *War and Peace in the Global Village* (1968)

Co-created by **Marshall McLuhan** and **Quentin Fiore**, this work diagnoses how electric media restructures society, drawing on Joyce's *Finnegans Wake*. Key themes include:
* **Referred Pain (p. 16):** How new media induces systemic psychological and physiological stress.
* **Computers and Extension of the Nervous System (p. 53):** Framing the computer as an extension of feedback circuitry and citing Joyce’s self-conception as an engineer.
* **Obsolescence of Institutional Hierarchies (p. 84):** Highlighting the burnout and vacancy of traditional leadership structures.
* **Archival and Cultural Gaps:** The book's Wikipedia and [Goodreads](https://goodreads.com) pages have historically suffered from deep neglect, though figures like **Terence McKenna** (1995) and analysts of non-linear information warfare—such as **Peter Pomerantsev** ([*The Atlantic*](https://theatlantic.com), 2014) and **Adam Curtis** (*HyperNormalisation*, 2016)—built upon these frameworks of manufactured confusion.

## 4. The 1968–1987 Musical Media-Ecology Soundtrack

Popular music has long tracked the shift from communal electronic discovery to digital numbness:
* **1968–1969:** Freeform FM radio, Joni Mitchell’s [*"Woodstock"*](https://youtube.com), and Blood, Sweat & Tears’ [*"Spinning Wheel"*](https://youtube.com).
* **1980:** Rush’s [*"The Spirit of Radio"*](https://youtube.com), documenting the commercial capture of open-format airwaves.
* **1987:** Pink Floyd’s [*"On the Turning Away"*](https://youtube.com), critiquing the passivity of screen-based spectator culture.
* **2018:** Chuck D’s [*"Celebration of Ignorance"*](https://youtube.com), echoing **Carl Sagan’s** *The Demon-Haunted World* (1995) regarding anti-intellectual spectacle.

## 5. Tactile Counter-Environments: Card Play, McGill, & *Pluribus*

Combating algorithmic isolation requires physical, face-to-face environments:
* **McGill University & Dr. Margie Golick:** McGill researcher Golick documented how fast, tactile card games like **Spit** (also known as **Slam**, [*Deal Me In!*](https://wikipedia.org), 1973) force active cognitive engagement.
* **Vince Gilligan’s *Pluribus* (Episode 8, "Charm Offensive"):** The NYU-trained creator (who studied under **Neil Postman** and **Dr. Eva Berger**) depicted protagonist Carol Sturka using the fast-paced game of Spit to resist a digital hive mind.
* **Community Reading Spaces:** Independent brick-and-mortar spaces like Austin’s [Monkeywrench Books](https://monkeywrenchbooks.org) foster analog community connection.
